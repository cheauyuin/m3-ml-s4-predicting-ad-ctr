Predicting Ad Click-Through Rate

Will a user click this ad — yes or no? A binary classification problem, and a case study in why the obvious metric (accuracy) is the wrong one.

40.4M ad impressions Avazu CTR dataset · 10 days Solution design focus
The Problem

Predict the click, before it happens

Every ad impression is a bet — the advertiser only earns if you click. We predict the probability of a click from context: site, device, time of day.

Why it's hard

Only ~17% of impressions get clicked. A model that always guesses "no click" would be 83% "accurate" — and completely useless.

Our decision: score with AUC and log-loss, never plain accuracy.

83% — No click
17% — Click
Solution Design

What we did, why, and the trade-off

What we didWhyTrade-off
Scored with AUC & log-loss Only 17% of impressions are clicked — accuracy would hide a useless model None — this is foundational, not negotiable
Time-based split (train d21-28, test d30) Random split hides overfitting; mirrors real "predict tomorrow" use Harder benchmark, lower-looking numbers
Frequency encoding for high-cardinality IDs Leak-safe (fit on train only), simple, fast Tested target encoding too — won by only +0.002 AUC, nearly doubled the overfit gap. Rejected it.
Trained LogReg and LightGBM Shows stability-vs-capacity as a real, measured trade-off LightGBM: +0.08 AUC, but less interpretable, needs tuning care
Ran the full 40M-row dataset +0.09 AUC vs. a 200k-row sample ~20-25 min runtime per run
